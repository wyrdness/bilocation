## Overview

Bilocation (also called multilocation) is the alleged ability of a person, or rarely an object, to be present in two distinct physical places simultaneously. The concept appears across many religious and esoteric traditions. In the Catholic Church, bilocation is recorded as a charism of the saints, with classic cases attributed to Saint Anthony of Padua, Saint Francis Xavier, Saint Martin de Porres, the Venerable María de Ágreda, and — most famously in the 20th century — Saint Padre Pio of Pietrelcina. In Hindu and Buddhist traditions, bilocation is one of the siddhis, ascribed in the Yoga Sutras of Patanjali. Parallel concepts appear in Theosophy, Sufism, Jewish mysticism, and shamanism. Catholic theologians distinguish between substantial bilocation (the saint physically present in two places) and apparitional bilocation (a non-substantial visible double appearing while the saint remains in one place). Skeptics, including Joe Nickell and the parapsychology critic Susan Blackmore, note that no case of bilocation has been verified under controlled conditions and that all evidence is anecdotal.

## Notable Sightings

### Saint Anthony of Padua at Limoges and Lisbon ⭐
> **Date**: 1227-01-01  
> **Location**: Limoges (France) and Lisbon (Portugal)  
> **Witnesses**: 0 (Members of two Franciscan communities (per hagiography))  
While preaching at Holy Thursday liturgy in Limoges, Anthony reportedly recalled a missed monastic obligation in another church across town (some traditions say in Lisbon), knelt, became motionless, and was simultaneously seen at the second site reading the appointed lesson before disappearing.
**Credibility**: low — Foundational Catholic bilocation episode.
**Sources**: Liber Miraculorum (medieval Franciscan); Wikipedia, Bilocation

### Sor María de Jesús de Ágreda — Jumano missionary bilocations ⭐
> **Date**: 1620-01-01  
> **Location**: Convent of the Conceptionists, Ágreda, Spain — to the Jumano people in present-day New Mexico/Texas  
> **Witnesses**: 0 (Franciscan missionaries (Alonso de Benavides), Jumano people (per Spanish report))  
Sor María, a Spanish Conceptionist nun who never left her cloister, reportedly experienced more than 500 mystical bilocations to evangelise the Jumano people of New Mexico between 1620 and 1623. Franciscan friars in the region, led by Alonso de Benavides, reported encountering Indigenous people who claimed to have been instructed by 'the Lady in Blue', whose description matched Sor María.
**Credibility**: low — One of the most cited and most studied bilocation cases.
**Sources**: Benavides, A. de (1630), Memorial; Wikipedia, María de Ágreda

### Alphonsus Liguori at the deathbed of Pope Clement XIV ⭐
> **Date**: 1774-09-22  
> **Location**: Arezzo (Tuscany) — Rome  
> **Witnesses**: 1 (Liguori himself, attendants, and (per hagiography) papal household)  
Saint Alphonsus Liguori reportedly fell into a long trance while preparing for Mass at Arezzo and, on awaking, said that he had been at the deathbed of Pope Clement XIV in Rome (about 200 km away). Witnesses at the papal court reportedly identified Liguori among those present.
**Credibility**: low — Frequently cited Counter-Reformation case.
**Sources**: Tannoia, A. (1798), Vita di Sant'Alfonso Maria de'Liguori; Catholic Encyclopedia

### Padre Pio in San Giovanni Rotondo and Montevideo ⭐
> **Date**: 1947-01-01  
> **Location**: San Giovanni Rotondo (Italy) — Montevideo (Uruguay)  
> **Witnesses**: 1 (Archbishop Antonio María Barbieri, Friars at San Giovanni Rotondo (per Capuchin testimony))  
Padre Pio is reported to have appeared at the bedside of the future Cardinal Antonio María Barbieri's friend in Uruguay while remaining at the Capuchin friary in San Giovanni Rotondo. Barbieri later identified Pio when he visited Italy.
**Credibility**: low — The signature 20th-century bilocation case.
**Sources**: Catholic biographies of Padre Pio; Wikipedia, Padre Pio

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Charism of the saints (Catholic)** _(Catholic mystical theology, Hagiographers (Tannoia, Bonaventura, Capuchin biographers))_

Bilocation is a divinely granted gift (charism) by which God permits a saint to appear simultaneously in two places, either substantially or apparitionally.

**Yogic siddhi** _(Patanjali, Modern yoga teachers)_

Bilocation is one of the powers attainable by an advanced practitioner of yoga, listed among the siddhis in Patanjali's Yoga Sutras.

**Astral projection / etheric double** _(H. P. Blavatsky, Sylvan Muldoon)_

Theosophical and modern occult traditions explain bilocation as projection of an etheric double (or astral body) while the physical body remains in place.

### Scientific

**Misidentification and coincidence** _(Joe Nickell, Benjamin Radford)_

Many cases reduce on examination to mistaken identity by witnesses, telescoped chronology in retelling, or coincidence between independent events.

**Hagiographic accretion** _(Hippolyte Delehaye (Bollandist), modern hagiography studies)_

Long after a saint's death, additional bilocation episodes accrete to the biography, expressing the community's theology of the saint's continuing presence.

### Skeptical

**No controlled evidence** _(Susan Blackmore, Joe Nickell)_

Despite a century of parapsychological interest, no case of bilocation has been replicated or evidenced under controlled conditions; all evidence is anecdotal.
